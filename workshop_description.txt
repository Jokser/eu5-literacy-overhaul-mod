[h1]Literacy Overhaul[/h1]

In vanilla, literacy is one number for everyone: a printing press or a new religion teaches peasants as much as priests. This mod gives every class its own literacy, so a medieval country has a learned elite and an illiterate countryside, as it historically did. Research depends on how well educated your elite is, and the rest of the game is adjusted so that the lower averages do not break it.

[h2]Class-stratified literacy[/h2]
[list]
[*][b]Nobles, clergy and burghers[/b] can reach high literacy. [b]Peasants, laborers and soldiers[/b] stay low until mass schooling: printed primers, Protestant Bible reading, secular education.
[*]Every advance, building, law, reform, privilege, religion, holy site and modifier that gave literacy to "everyone" has been reviewed. Bonuses now go to the classes that actually benefited from them: academies, libraries and courts educate the elite, schools and cheap books reach the common people.
[*]Holy sites no longer give up to +50 literacy, and theatres and academies no longer teach a whole country's peasantry.
[/list]

[h2]Print and mass reading[/h2]
[list]
[*]The [b]printing press[/b] educates the elite, especially burghers. [b]Printed Primers[/b], a cheap follow-up advance, bring reading to the common people and double literacy growth.
[*]In the Muslim world printed primers come only in the age of absolutism (the first Ottoman press opened in 1727). [b]Madrasas[/b] educate the ulema and the urban elite, not the villages.
[*][b]Newspapers[/b] and the [b]Enlightenment court[/b] raise literacy among nobles, burghers and the common people in the late game.
[/list]

[h2]Religions and cultures[/h2]
[list]
[*][b]Catholic[/b] clergy are highly literate. [b]Orthodox[/b] clergy rely on monastic book culture; Russians catch up with the [b]Stoglav council[/b] in the age of Reformation, and the Greek aristocracy has its own [b]Byzantine paideia[/b].
[*][b]Protestants[/b] get it in the religion itself, not in aspects the AI never picks. [b]Lutherans[/b] teach the laity from the Reformation and become the most literate church after the [b]church reading examinations[/b] of the age of absolutism. Anglicans have Oxbridge clergy and grammar schools, Calvinists parish schools.
[*][b]Islam[/b] gives its ulema a literacy base; each Muslim school adds its own. Hindu Brahmins and Jain merchants, Theravada village monasteries, Armenian vardapets, Chinese scholar-gentry and the Maya scribes all have their own profiles.
[/list]

[h2]Learned Elite: a new research bonus[/h2]
Vanilla research from literacy uses the [i]average[/i] literacy of all pops, which is mostly the peasants'.
[list]
[*]A new [b]Learned Elite[/b] modifier adds research based on the literacy of your [b]nobles (40%), clergy (35%) and burghers (25%)[/b]. Each estate counts by how educated it is, not by how many people it has, so Education of the Court, noble advances and clerical privileges all matter.
[*]The vanilla average-literacy bonus is kept. Each embraced institution gives +4% research instead of +5% and average control counts half, so education makes up about half of the research bonus. Simulated on saves from 1450 to 1800, no age becomes noticeably faster or slower.
[*]Recalculated once a year in a single pass over the pops.
[/list]

[h2]Keeping the rest of the game intact[/h2]
[list]
[*][b]Institution spread[/b] depends on local literacy, so it is compensated: institutions spread at about the vanilla pace in Europe, and more slowly where literacy was historically low.
[*]Demand for books and paper, the literacy bonus to peasant promotion, the Royal Society decision, the sola scriptura events, the revolution disasters and the Catholic scholars' zeal are adjusted to the lower averages.
[*]The AI now picks the elite literacy advances and the new primers instead of skipping them for a century, and Muscovy leaves uneducated masses after the Stoglav council.
[/list]

[h2]Universities[/h2]
[list]
[*]A university needs a developed town (development 30+) with highly literate clergy (85+) and burghers (25+). It gives a big literacy boost to all three upper classes and costs more to build and maintain.
[*]Loopholes that built universities anywhere are closed: the cheap Orthodox event option and parliament sponsorship.
[/list]

[h2]Compatibility[/h2]
Most changes replace single entries (REPLACE_OR_CREATE), not whole files, so mods that do not touch literacy are compatible. Mods that change the same advances, buildings, laws or religions will conflict. Five files are replaced whole, because the game cannot edit them entry by entry: [i]script_values/institution_spread.txt[/i], [i]events/religion/church_aspects.txt[/i], [i]events/religion/orthodox_flavor.txt[/i], [i]parliament_issues/01_country_specific_parliament_issues.txt[/i] and [i]parliament_issues/05_burghers_estate_parliament_issues.txt[/i]. Other mods that replace these files conflict.

[b]Ruthenia and Steppe Fix (RSF):[/b] RSF redefines the Orthodox and Miaphysite religions with TRY_REPLACE, which overrides other mods whatever the load order. To get this mod's literacy for them with RSF, also use [b]Literacy overhaul - RSF compatibility[/b] and load it after both mods. Literacy overhaul itself does not need RSF.

Based in part on the literacy changes of Flavorium Universalis Balance.
